PNT's greatest downside, its higher required computational power, is an issue with a necessarily
bright future. It is unlikely that consumer-grade computers will require more than sixty-five thousand
ports even moving into the future, since regardless of technological developments, the average
computer-owner will not be attempting thousands of computations running on thousands of different services
simultaneously. In contrast, the demand for improvements in computer hardware computing power has been nonstop
in the last few decades, and its results have been staggering. It is, after all, more realistic to expect
consumers to desire more speed for the few tasks they need to complete with their machines, rather than
the capacity for more tasks. With this in mind, a computational-significant system such as PNT, whose efficiency
relies on the ratio of SPEED:PORTS, will only improve in usability, as a computer's overall SPEED will always
be increasing, in comparison to the already mostly satisfactory number of PORTS.
